Why Cold Pressed

Refining strips the oil. We simply don't refine.

Commercial oil is pulled out of the seed with solvent at high temperature, then bleached and deodorised until nothing of the seed survives but fat. Cold pressing keeps the temperature low, so what stays behind is what the seed put there.

01

Low temperature

The press turns slowly. Without frictional heat, the oil's natural antioxidants and vitamin E survive the pressing.

02

Nothing added

No hexane, no caustic wash, no bleaching earth, no synthetic antioxidants. The ingredient list on every Nuova label is one line long.

03

Real aroma

Coconut oil that smells of coconut. Mustard oil with its bite. Sesame that smells of til. That aroma is exactly what refining removes.

How It's Made

Four steps, and nothing in between

01

A1 grade seed

Seed and copra bought on grade, then cleaned and dried. Anything damp, broken or off-colour is rejected before it reaches the press.

02

Cold pressing

Slow, low-RPM pressing that keeps the oil cool, so none of what makes the oil worth buying is cooked out of it.

03

Settling & filtering

The oil rests and settles naturally, then passes through cloth. No chemical clarifying, which is why a little sediment is normal.

04

Bottled & coded

Filled, sealed, and marked with batch number and packing date. Best before six months from that date.

Questions

Things customers ask us

Why does cold pressed oil cost more?

A cold press pulls less oil out of the same seed than a solvent plant does, and it runs slowly. You are paying for a lower yield and a longer process — not for the bottle.

My coconut oil has turned solid. Is it spoiled?

No. Pure coconut oil sets below roughly 24 °C and melts again in warmth. Stand the bottle in warm water for a few minutes. Refined coconut oil that stays liquid through a Pune winter is the one worth questioning.

Why is there sediment at the bottom?

That is fine seed particle, and it is evidence the oil was not bleached or chemically clarified. Shake gently before use, or let it settle and pour off the top.

How long does it keep?

Best before six months from the packing date printed on the bottle. Store in a dry place away from direct heat and light, with the cap closed. Flaxseed oil is the most delicate of the four — finish it sooner and keep it cool.

Which of these can I deep fry in?

Coconut, sesame and mustard oil all handle Indian frying well. Do not deep fry in flaxseed oil, and avoid reusing any frying oil more than once.
